Expression test

Measures how well expression reading works on your own face and webcam. You're asked to make each of the 7 expressions for a few seconds, then to talk for a few seconds and to rest your chin on your hand (to measure the talking and hand detectors). Only numbers are saved (the expression asked for, the model's scores, face-shape cues, body points and talking signals; no pictures, no audio) in data\expr_eval. Run tools\expr_eval.py to see the results.

Feeling check

The only way to learn whether any of these signals track how you actually feel: now and then you say how you feel (two 1-7 scales). tools\expr_eval.py compares the answers with the signals and, once there are enough, fits valence/arousal to you.

Display

Expression is read from sharp, camera-facing frames and averaged over the last second. With calibration on, each named person's usual look is learned over about a minute; a reading that departs from it is highlighted, one that matches it is marked "usual". Calibration also learns each person's normal for every other signal (posture, movement, voice, pulse...), alone and in company, over a few minutes, and the evidence shows what is unusual for them. All of it stays in this browser.

Voice is analysed live and never recorded: loudness, pitch, speaking rate, and the voice model's arousal/valence. Pulse reads tiny skin-colour changes and needs a still face in steady light. Body posture adds head height, shoulders, lean, body movement, breathing, hand on face and crossed arms. The "skip" and "state model" options change the expression reading and aren't measured yet; run the Expression test and tools\expr_eval.py first.
